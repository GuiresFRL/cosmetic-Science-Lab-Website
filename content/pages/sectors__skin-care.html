The science

The skin biology we formulate for.

Three areas drive most of the skin care briefs we receive. For each one we choose the actives, design the base and plan the evidence together.

Barrier health

A stronger barrier, calmer skin.

The outer layer of skin keeps water in and irritants out. When it is disrupted, skin turns dry, tight and rough, and reacts more easily. Barrier-support products are one of the most requested briefs we see.

Ingredients CSL scientists work with

CeramidesCholesterolFree fatty acidsNiacinamidePanthenolSqualanePhytosterols

How CSL proves it works

  • TEWLTransepidermal water loss before and after use
  • Tape-strip challengeRecovery after a controlled barrier stress
  • Sensitive-skin panelsTolerance on self-declared sensitive skin
Published evidenceLueangarun 2019 · ceramidesBissett 2005 · niacinamideMarini 2014 · ectoin
Formulation note

Lipid ratio matters as much as the lipids. We balance ceramides, cholesterol and fatty acids close to skin’s own ratio, in a lamellar or cream-gel base that holds them.

Microbiome balance

Working with skin’s own ecosystem.

A diverse community of microbes lives on healthy skin and helps defend it. Harsh surfactants, high pH and aggressive preservation can upset that balance. We formulate so products clean and protect without stripping.

Ingredients CSL scientists work with

Prebiotic sugarsPostbiotic fermentsLactobacillus lysatesInulinMild amino-acid surfactantspH-balanced bases

How CSL proves it works

  • Microbiome swabsSequencing of skin flora before and after use
  • In-vitro selectivityEffect on beneficial versus unwanted strains
  • “Microbiome-friendly” wordingChecked against what the data supports in each market
Published evidenceGueniche 2008 · postbiotic
Formulation note

Preservation and microbiome claims pull in opposite directions. We pick a preservative system that passes ISO 11930 at the lowest effective level, then test the finished product on skin.

Water balance & hydration

Hydration that lasts beyond the first hour.

Moisturisation stays at the top of shoppers’ wish lists. Lasting hydration needs more than a humectant: skin also relies on small molecules called osmolytes to hold water inside its cells, and on a sealed surface to stop it escaping.

Ingredients CSL scientists work with

GlycerinBetaineEctoinHyaluronic acid (multi-weight)UreaSodium PCATrehalose

How CSL proves it works

  • CorneometerSkin hydration at 1, 8, 24 and 72 hours
  • TEWLWhether moisture is retained, not just added
  • Consumer perceptionSelf-assessed comfort and softness over 2–4 weeks
Published evidencePavicic 2011 · hyaluronic acidBreternitz 2008 · glycerin
Formulation note

A 24-hour or 72-hour hydration claim needs kinetic measurement, not a single reading. We set the time points before the study so the claim and the data match.

Clinical evidence

What the published trials show.

Peer-reviewed human studies behind the ingredients and approaches on this page. Each links to its PubMed record.

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · retinol

0.4% retinol lotion vs vehicle

Randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled · 36 older adults · 24 weeks

Fine wrinkling improved significantly vs vehicle (score change −1.64 vs −0.08, P<.001), with increased glycosaminoglycans and procollagen I.

Kafi R et al. Arch Dermatol 2007;143(5):606–612 · PMID 17515510 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · niacinamide

5% niacinamide vs vehicle, split-face

Double-blind, split-face · 50 women · 12 weeks

Significant reductions in fine lines, wrinkles, spots, red blotchiness and sallowness, and improved elasticity, vs the vehicle side.

Bissett DL et al. Dermatol Surg 2005;31(7 Pt 2):860–865 · PMID 16029679 ↗

Controlled trialEven tone · niacinamide

5% niacinamide vs vehicle; 2% niacinamide + sunscreen vs sunscreen

Vehicle-controlled human trials · 18 and 120 participants

Significantly less hyperpigmentation and lighter-looking skin vs vehicle, visible from 4 weeks.

Hakozaki T et al. Br J Dermatol 2002;147(1):20–31 · PMID 12100180 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · vitamin C

5% vitamin C cream vs vehicle

Double-blind, randomised, split-site · 20 women · 6 months

Clinical photoageing score improved from 6.7 to 4.4 on the treated side, with denser skin microrelief and fewer deep furrows.

Humbert PG et al. Exp Dermatol 2003;12(3):237–244 · PMID 12823436 ↗

Randomised controlled trialHydration · hyaluronic acid

0.1% hyaluronic acid creams (50–2000 kDa) vs vehicle

Randomised, controlled, intra-individual · 76 women · 60 days

All molecular weights improved hydration and elasticity vs vehicle; the low weights (50 and 130 kDa) also reduced wrinkle depth.

Pavicic T et al. J Drugs Dermatol 2011;10(9):990–1000 · PMID 22052267 ↗

Randomised controlled trialHydration · glycerin

20% glycerol emollient vs vehicle

Double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomised · 24 adults with atopic dermatitis · 4 weeks

Skin hydration rose vs vehicle from week 1 (p<0.0001) and barrier function (TEWL) improved by week 4.

Breternitz M et al. Skin Pharmacol Physiol 2008;21(1):39–45 · PMID 18025807 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBarrier · ceramides

Ceramide cream vs hydrophilic cream, split-body

Double-blind, randomised · 24 adults with dry skin · 28 days + 7-day follow-up

Better hydration, lower TEWL and improved skin pH at every time point, maintained 7 days after stopping.

Lueangarun S et al. Dermatol Ther 2019;32(6):e13090 · PMID 31585489 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMicrobiome · postbiotic

5% Vitreoscilla filiformis lysate cream vs vehicle

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled · 75 people with atopic dermatitis · 30 days

Reduced eczema severity (P=0.0044), itch and sleep loss, and lowered S. aureus colonisation vs placebo.

Gueniche A et al. Br J Dermatol 2008;159(6):1357–1363 · PMID 18795916 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBarrier · ectoin

Ectoin cream vs a non-steroidal barrier cream

Randomised, double-blind, intra-individual, multicentre · 65 adults · 28 days

Efficacy and tolerability equivalent to the active comparator on eczema score, global assessment and itch.

Marini A et al. Skin Pharmacol Physiol 2014;27(2):57–65 · PMID 23949258 ↗

Randomised controlled trialPhotoageing · daily sunscreen

Daily broad-spectrum SPF 15+ vs discretionary use

Randomised community trial, blinded assessment · 903 adults · 4.5 years

Daily users showed 24% less skin ageing (relative odds 0.76) than discretionary users.

Hughes MC et al. Ann Intern Med 2013;158(11):781–790 · PMID 23732711 ↗

FAQ

Skin care questions.

Can you develop a barrier-repair or microbiome-friendly product?

Yes. We formulate around barrier lipids, prebiotics and postbiotics and gentle surfactant systems, then support the claim with TEWL, sensitive-skin panels or microbiome swab studies.

Can you match the texture of a product we already sell or like?

Yes. We benchmark the reference product with sensory panels and rheology, then build a formula that matches or improves on it without copying its formula.

Do you supply the fragrance?

We work with fragrance-house partners to brief, select and test a scent for your product, or use one you already have. Every fragrance is checked against IFRA limits and allergen labelling rules.

Can you make a natural or COSMOS-certified skin care product?

Yes. We screen raw materials for the standard you choose, calculate natural-origin content under ISO 16128 and prepare the documents your certifier asks for.

Who owns the skin care formula?

You do. We sign an NDA before you share your brief, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end of the project.
