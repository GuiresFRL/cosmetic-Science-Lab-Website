The science

What halal cosmetics require.

Halal status covers ingredients, processing and cross-contamination. It sits alongside, not instead of, normal cosmetic safety rules.

Ingredient sourcing

Every ingredient, traced to its source.

Some common cosmetic ingredients can come from animal sources: glycerin, fatty acids, collagen, gelatin, keratin and some emulsifiers. We choose plant, mineral or synthetic sources, or halal-certified animal sources, and collect certificates for each raw material.

Formulation note

Hidden sources matter: processing aids, carriers and fragrance components can all introduce non-halal materials. We check the full supply chain, not just the INCI list.

Analytical verification

Proof by laboratory testing.

Certificates can be backed with laboratory screening for porcine material. Research shows cosmetic matrices are challenging, so combining methods such as real-time PCR and ELISA, or using LC-MS/MS, gives more reliable results.

Certification

A smooth route to certification.

Certification is market-specific. Indonesia’s BPJPH, Malaysia’s JAKIM and GCC authorities each recognise particular certifiers. We prepare the formula documentation, production controls and cross-contamination measures the certifier will audit.

FAQ

Halal cosmetics questions.

Is halal certification required in Indonesia?

Yes. Cosmetics sold in Indonesia must be halal-certified or carry the required non-halal statement from 17 October 2026, under Government Regulation 42/2024.

Will a Malaysian or GCC certificate be accepted in Indonesia?

Only if the certifier is recognised by BPJPH, and the certificate must be registered before sale. We check recognition for each market.

Can you test for porcine material?

Yes, using real-time PCR, ELISA or LC-MS/MS depending on the product.

Who owns the formula?

You do. We sign an NDA first, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end.
