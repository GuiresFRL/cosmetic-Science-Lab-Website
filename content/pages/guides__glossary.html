Technical guide

Cosmetic science glossary: the technical terms behind every product

Plain-language definitions of the formulation, testing, clinical and regulatory terms Guires CSL scientists use. Each term has its own page with the standard or regulation behind it and where we put it into practice.

Labelling & formulationActivesTestingClinicalRegulatoryQuality

Labelling & formulation

INCI (International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients)

The standard ingredient names used on cosmetic labels in the EU, US, ASEAN and many other markets, assigned by the INCI committee of the Personal Care Products Council.

Read more →

ISO 16128

The ISO guidelines on technical definitions and criteria for natural and organic cosmetic ingredients (Part 1, 2016) and the way to calculate natural and organic origin indexes for ingredients and products (Part 2, 2017).

Read more →

pH

A measure of acidity or alkalinity on a scale of 0 to 14.

Read more →

Emulsion

A dispersion of oil and water held together by emulsifiers, either oil-in-water (most lotions and creams) or water-in-oil (richer, water-resistant textures).

Read more →

Rheology

How a product flows and deforms: viscosity, yield stress and shear thinning.

Read more →

Humectants, emollients and occlusives

The three ways a moisturiser works.

Read more →

Surfactant

A surface-active ingredient that lets oil and water mix, so it can cleanse, foam or emulsify.

Read more →

Actives

AHAs and BHA

Alpha hydroxy acids such as glycolic and lactic acid are water-soluble exfoliants that loosen dead surface cells; the beta hydroxy acid salicylic acid is oil-soluble and works inside pores.

Read more →

Retinoids

Vitamin A derivatives used in cosmetics, including retinol, retinal and retinyl esters.

Read more →

Peptides

Short chains of amino acids used as cosmetic actives, such as signal peptides that target the look of fine lines.

Read more →

Testing

Stability testing

Storage of a product at controlled temperatures, humidity and light to check that it keeps its look, smell, texture, pH and active content over its shelf life.

Read more →

Challenge test (preservative efficacy)

A test in which a product is deliberately contaminated with set microorganisms to check that its preservative system reduces them fast enough.

Read more →

In vitro testing and NAMs

Laboratory tests on cells, tissues or chemistry instead of animals, often called new approach methodologies.

Read more →

Clinical

HRIPT (Human Repeat Insult Patch Test)

A supervised patch test on volunteers with repeated applications over about three weeks, a rest period and a final challenge, used to check that a product does not cause irritation or sensitisation under exaggerated use..

Read more →

TEWL (transepidermal water loss)

The rate at which water evaporates through the stratum corneum, measured with instruments such as a Tewameter.

Read more →

Corneometer

An instrument that measures skin surface hydration by electrical capacitance, used to substantiate moisturising claims such as 24-hour hydration..

Read more →

SPF (Sun Protection Factor)

A measure of protection against the UVB rays that cause sunburn.

Read more →

UVA protection

Protection against the longer UV rays linked to skin ageing.

Read more →

Regulatory

PIF (Product Information File)

The technical file required under Article 11 of the EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009, held by the Responsible Person for ten years after the last batch is placed on the market.

Read more →

CPNP (Cosmetic Products Notification Portal)

The EU online portal where every cosmetic must be notified before it is placed on the market, under Article 13 of Regulation 1223/2009..

Read more →

Responsible Person

The legal or natural person, established in the EU (or in Great Britain for the UK), who is responsible for making sure each cosmetic complies with the regulation, holds the PIF and notifies the product..

Read more →

Margin of Safety (MoS)

The ratio between a toxicological point of departure, such as a NOAEL, and the systemic exposure from using the product.

Read more →

NOAEL

No Observed Adverse Effect Level: the highest dose in a study at which no adverse effect was seen.

Read more →

CMR substances

Substances classified as carcinogenic, mutagenic or toxic for reproduction.

Read more →

MoCRA

The US Modernization of Cosmetics Regulation Act of 2022, which added facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation and adverse event reporting duties for cosmetics sold in the US..

Read more →

Quality

ISO 22716 (cosmetic GMP)

The international guidelines on Good Manufacturing Practice for cosmetics, covering premises, equipment, raw materials, production, quality control, documentation and complaints..

Read more →
