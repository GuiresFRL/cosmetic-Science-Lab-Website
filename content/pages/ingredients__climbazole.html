Climbazole: what the evidence shows for dandruff and flaking scalp

Climbazole is an azole antifungal used in anti-dandruff shampoos, usually alongside another active. Here is what the trials show and how CSL formulation scientists use it within the EU limits.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Moderate

At a glance

What it is
An azole antifungal used in anti-dandruff shampoos and as a preservative
Main benefits
Fewer visible flakes and less itch, especially when combined with another anti-dandruff active
Levels tested in trials
0.45% to 0.65%, mostly with zinc pyrithione or piroctone olamine
Time to results
4 to 5 weeks in clinical studies
Watch for
EU limit of 2% in rinse-off anti-dandruff shampoo, lower preservative limits, few recent trials

01What is it?

Climbazole is an azole antifungal, related to ketoconazole, used in cosmetic anti-dandruff shampoos. It is rarely used alone; most products pair it with piroctone olamine or, outside the EU, zinc pyrithione. It can also act as a preservative at lower levels.

02What it does

Like other azoles, climbazole targets Malassezia, the scalp yeast linked to dandruff. In shampoos it reduces visible flakes and itch over a few weeks. Combination products appear to outperform single actives, but formula design matters as much as the actives chosen.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceModerate: a few randomised and half-head trials, mostly older, industry-run and testing combinations

CSL verdict

Climbazole helps reduce visible dandruff, and adding it to another active can boost results. But the trials are small or old, mostly company-funded, and one large trial showed an optimised single-active shampoo beating a climbazole combination. Keep claims to "helps reduce visible flakes" and support them with testing on the finished product.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐For anti-dandruff use in the EU, the limit is 2% in rinse-off shampoo; trials used 0.45% to 0.65%.
  • ☐As a preservative, the EU limits are lower: 0.5% in rinse-off shampoo and 0.2% in face creams, hair lotions and foot care. Annex V lists no other product types for preservative use.
  • ☐Combine with piroctone olamine for a broader antifungal effect, and check deposition and in-use performance of the full formula.
  • ☐Include the Annex III label warnings where required and check scalp tolerance; mild irritation is possible.

05Regulatory and claims

Commission Regulation (EU) 2019/698 sets climbazole limits in Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009: Annex III entry 310 allows up to 2% in rinse-off anti-dandruff shampoo; Annex V entry 32 allows up to 0.2% in hair lotions, face creams and foot care and 0.5% in rinse-off shampoo as a preservative. These follow SCCS opinion SCCS/1590/17. Climbazole is not in the US OTC dandruff monograph (21 CFR 358.710), so US drug claims cannot rest on it. CSL regulatory specialists confirm the status in each other target market.

06Questions brands ask

Is climbazole better than piroctone olamine?

One randomised trial found a climbazole shampoo cleared dandruff in more users than the same base with piroctone olamine (80% vs 55%). Most brands combine the two.

Can climbazole be used in leave-on scalp products?

In the EU only as a preservative, up to 0.2% in hair lotions. The 2% anti-dandruff limit applies only to rinse-off shampoo.

Is climbazole a good zinc pyrithione replacement in the EU?

It is one of the main options, usually with piroctone olamine. Performance depends on the whole formula, so test the finished shampoo.
