Cosmetic clays: what the evidence shows for oil control and masks

Kaolin, bentonite and other clays soak up surface oil and give masks their texture. CSL formulation scientists explain what human studies show, why the effect is short-lived and why raw-material purity matters.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Moderate

At a glance

What it is
Natural mineral powders such as kaolin, bentonite (montmorillonite), illite (green clay) and rhassoul, often used with silica to absorb oil
Main benefits
Less visible shine straight after use, a cleaner-feeling and matter-looking skin, mask and cleanser texture
Levels tested in trials
Up to 21% total clay (7% each of three clays) in one mask study; most studies test whole products with undisclosed levels
Time to results
Immediate for shine; 4 to 8 weeks for blemish-related results in small studies of combination products
Watch for
Heavy metals (lead, arsenic), microbial load, dryness, and oil returning within hours

01What is it?

Cosmetic clays are fine mineral powders mined from natural deposits. Kaolin is mild and white; bentonite and montmorillonite are swelling clays that hold water and oil; green clay is usually illite or a mix; rhassoul is a Moroccan clay. Silica is often added alongside them as an oil-absorbing, mattifying powder.

02What it does

Clays have a large surface area and, in swelling clays, layers that can take up water, oils and charged substances. Kaolin also gives a gentle polishing action. On skin this shows mainly as less shine and a fresher feel straight after a mask or cleanser, with oil returning over the following hours.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceModerate: consistent short-term oil reduction, but most human studies are uncontrolled or test clay mixed with other actives

CSL verdict

Clays reliably reduce visible shine for a few hours, which supports claims such as "absorbs excess oil" and "leaves skin looking matte". Longer-term blemish results come from small or uncontrolled studies of products that also contain other actives, so claims about blemishes should be modest and backed by testing of the finished product.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Kaolin suits gentle masks and cleansers; bentonite and other smectites swell, thicken and absorb more oil but can feel more drying. CSL formulation scientists balance clay type and level with humectants and emollients so skin does not feel tight.
  • ☐Raw-material purity is the main safety issue. Natural clays can carry lead, arsenic, mercury and other metals, and crystalline silica or asbestos fibres can occur in some mineral deposits, so each lot is bought to specification with certificates of analysis and, where needed, independent testing.
  • ☐Clays come from the ground and can carry high microbial counts. Treated (for example heat-treated or irradiated) grades, microbial limits on the raw material and a robust preservative system are needed, especially in water-based masks.
  • ☐For powder formats such as dry shampoo or setting powders, CSL formulation scientists control particle size and dust, and favour amorphous silica grades for mattifying and oil absorption.

05Regulatory and claims

Kaolin, bentonite and silica are widely used cosmetic ingredients, but contaminants drive their regulation. The US FDA recommends no more than 10 ppm lead in externally applied cosmetics (draft guidance), and in 2016 it warned consumers not to use a bentonite clay product found to contain 37.5 ppm lead. A US proposed rule on asbestos testing of talc-containing cosmetics, required under MoCRA, was withdrawn in November 2025, with a new proposal promised. CSL regulatory specialists confirm limits for each target market, and keep claims cosmetic: "absorbs excess oil", not "treats acne".

06Questions brands ask

Do clay masks shrink pores or control oil long term?

Not on current evidence. Studies show less oil straight after use, but levels return within hours. Pores can look less noticeable while the skin is less oily.

Which clay is best for oily skin?

Swelling clays such as bentonite absorb more oil than kaolin but can be more drying. Many products blend them to balance oil control and comfort.

Are natural clays safe?

They can be, if the raw material is tested. Clays can contain heavy metals and microbes, and one 2025 study found arsenic above the US colour-additive limit in a black clay. Supplier testing and specifications are essential.
