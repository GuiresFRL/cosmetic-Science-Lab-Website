The science

Why skin reacts, and how CSL scientists calm it.

Sensitive skin usually combines a weaker barrier with easily triggered nerves and inflammation. CSL formulation scientists formulate to support the barrier and remove triggers.

Soothing actives

Calmer skin, less redness.

Colloidal oatmeal, licorice and postbiotic lysates have controlled trials showing reduced redness, itch and irritation. We combine them with barrier support.

Formulation note

Fewer ingredients means fewer triggers. We remove fragrance, limit preservatives and choose low-irritation emulsifiers.

Barrier support

Fewer reactions, stronger skin.

A stronger barrier lets fewer irritants in. Ceramides, glycerin and ectoin all have randomised trials showing better barrier function or tolerance, with glycerin stinging less than urea.

Clinical evidence

What the published trials show.

Peer-reviewed human studies behind the actives and approaches on this page. Each links to its record.

Randomised controlled trialEczema-prone skin · colloidal oatmeal

1% colloidal oatmeal cream vs prescription barrier cream

Randomised, double-blind, active-controlled · children with eczema · 3 weeks

The oatmeal cream matched the prescription barrier cream on eczema improvement, with similar tolerability.

Manufacturer-sponsored.

Lisante TA et al. J Dermatolog Treat 2017;28(7):659–667 · PMID 28366039 ↗

Controlled trialSoothing · licorice

1% and 2% licorice extract gel vs base gel

Controlled three-group trial · 90 adults with eczema · 2 weeks

The 2% gel reduced redness, swelling and itch more than the 1% gel.

Saeedi M et al. J Dermatolog Treat 2003;14(3):153–157 · PMID 14522625 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMicrobiome · postbiotic

5% Vitreoscilla filiformis lysate cream vs vehicle

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled · 75 people with atopic dermatitis · 30 days

Reduced eczema severity (P=0.0044), itch and sleep loss, and lowered S. aureus colonisation vs placebo.

Gueniche A et al. Br J Dermatol 2008;159(6):1357–1363 · PMID 18795916 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBarrier · ceramides

Ceramide cream vs hydrophilic cream, split-body

Double-blind, randomised · 24 adults with dry skin · 28 days + 7-day follow-up

Better hydration, lower TEWL and improved skin pH at every time point, maintained 7 days after stopping.

Lueangarun S et al. Dermatol Ther 2019;32(6):e13090 · PMID 31585489 ↗

Randomised controlled trialDry skin · glycerin vs urea

Glycerin cream vs urea-saline cream

Randomised, double-blind · 197 adults with atopic dermatitis · 30 days

Both relieved dryness equally; the glycerin cream caused less stinging (10% vs 24% moderate–severe).

Lodén M et al. Acta Derm Venereol 2002;82(1):45–47 · PMID 12013198 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBarrier · ectoin

Ectoin cream vs a non-steroidal barrier cream

Randomised, double-blind, intra-individual, multicentre · 65 adults · 28 days

Efficacy and tolerability equivalent to the active comparator on eczema score, global assessment and itch.

Marini A et al. Skin Pharmacol Physiol 2014;27(2):57–65 · PMID 23949258 ↗

These are published studies on ingredients, methods and routines, not on products we have made. A finished product needs its own testing before a claim goes on pack, and we design that study with you. See the full evidence library

FAQ

Sensitive skin & redness questions.

What does “hypoallergenic” mean?

It has no legal definition. We support it with HRIPT data and a formula free of common allergens, and word it carefully.

How do you test on sensitive skin?

We recruit panels with self-declared or stinging-test-confirmed sensitive skin and run in-use studies under dermatologist supervision.

Can sensitive-skin products contain fragrance?

They can, but fragrance is a common trigger. Most sensitive ranges are fragrance-free.

Who owns the formula?

You do. We sign an NDA before you share your brief, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end of the project.
