Bakuchiol: what the evidence shows as a gentler retinol alternative

Bakuchiol is a plant-derived active often sold as a gentle alternative to retinol. Here is what the trials show, why the evidence is still thin, and how CSL formulation scientists use it.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Moderate

At a glance

What it is
A plant compound from the seeds of Psoralea corylifolia (babchi); not a retinoid, though it affects some of the same skin pathways
Main benefits
Fewer visible fine lines and a more even-looking tone, with less irritation than retinol in one trial
Levels tested in trials
0.5% twice daily in the key controlled trial; 1.5% in an open-label combination serum
Time to results
8 to 12 weeks in published studies
Watch for
Small, mostly industry-funded evidence base; many studies test combination products

01What is it?

Bakuchiol is an oily compound extracted from babchi seeds, a plant used in traditional Indian and Chinese medicine. It is not related to vitamin A in structure. It became popular as a ‘retinol alternative’ after lab work showed it switches on some of the same genes as retinol.

02What it does

In lab studies bakuchiol changes gene activity in a retinol-like way, including genes linked to collagen. On skin, the best trial found fewer visible wrinkles and less uneven pigmentation after 12 weeks, similar to retinol but with less scaling and stinging. It is stable in light, which makes it easier to formulate than retinol.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceModerate: one well-designed randomised trial against retinol, with most other studies open-label and uncontrolled

CSL verdict

Bakuchiol has one good randomised trial showing results similar to 0.5% retinol with better tolerance, and little else of high quality. It suits gentle, sensitive-skin or ‘retinol-alternative’ positioning, with modest claims such as ‘visibly smooths the look of fine lines’ supported by testing of the finished product. It should not be described as ‘natural retinol’ or as equal to retinol in general.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐The best-tested level is 0.5%, applied twice daily. CSL formulation scientists keep to tested levels and support any higher-strength claims with product-specific testing.
  • ☐Bakuchiol is oil-soluble and light-stable, so it works well in emulsions, oils and serums with an oil phase, and in daytime products.
  • ☐It pairs well with barrier and soothing ingredients, and can be combined with low-level retinoids in ‘gentle retinoid’ ranges.
  • ☐As with any new active, CSL formulation scientists run repeat-insult patch testing on the finished product and keep supplier purity specifications on file.

05Regulatory and claims

CSL regulatory specialists could not confirm a specific concentration limit for bakuchiol in the EU Cosmetics Regulation annexes, and confirm its status in each target market before launch. Because it is a plant-derived compound, supplier documentation on purity and origin is reviewed for each batch source. Claims stay cosmetic: ‘improves the look of fine lines’, not ‘repairs photodamage’.

06Questions brands ask

Is bakuchiol as good as retinol?

In one 12-week randomised trial of 44 people, 0.5% bakuchiol twice daily performed similarly to 0.5% retinol once daily, with less irritation. That is one small study, so ‘as good as retinol’ is too strong a general claim.

Is bakuchiol suitable for sensitive skin?

It caused less scaling and stinging than retinol in the key trial, which supports gentle positioning. That does not make it irritation-free for everyone, so the finished product still needs patch testing.

Can bakuchiol be used with retinol?

Yes. Some commercial products combine them, but most combination studies have no control group, so claims should reflect the tested product rather than each ingredient.
