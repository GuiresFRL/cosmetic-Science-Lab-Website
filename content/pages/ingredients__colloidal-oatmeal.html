Colloidal oatmeal: what the evidence shows for dry, itchy-feeling skin

Finely milled oats are one of the few botanicals recognised by the US FDA as a skin protectant. Here is what trials show for dryness and itch, and how CSL formulation scientists use colloidal oatmeal.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Good

At a glance

What it is
Oat kernels ground to a fine powder that disperses in water; contains starches, beta-glucan, lipids and avenanthramides
Main benefits
Relief of dryness and itch; better hydration and barrier in dry, eczema-prone skin
Levels tested in trials
1% colloidal oatmeal cream in most recent trials; US OTC minimum 0.007%
Time to results
Days for itch and comfort; 2 to 6 weeks for dryness and eczema scores
Watch for
Known oat allergy; microbial quality of a natural powder; OTC rules if drug claims are made in the US

01What is it?

Colloidal oatmeal is whole oat kernel milled so finely that it stays suspended in water. It is used in creams, lotions, washes and bath soaks. In the US it is an approved over-the-counter skin protectant.

02What it does

Oat starches and beta-glucan hold water and form a protective film, oat lipids support the barrier, and avenanthramides calm inflammation and itch. In trials this shows as better hydration, less dryness and itch, and eczema scores similar to a prescription barrier cream.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceGood: several randomised trials and a 2026 meta-analysis, though trials are small and mostly compare against other creams

CSL verdict

Colloidal oatmeal reliably improves hydration and comfort in dry and eczema-prone skin and is very well tolerated. Its effect on eczema scores matches barrier creams rather than medicines. Cosmetic wording such as "soothes dry, itchy-feeling skin" and "suitable for eczema-prone skin" fits; "relieves itching due to eczema" is a US OTC drug claim.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Recent trials use 1% in creams; this is a useful benchmark. The US OTC minimum of 0.007% applies only to monograph skin protectant products.
  • ☐Disperse the powder fully in the water phase and check viscosity; high levels thicken and can feel gritty. It suits creams, lotions, washes and bath soaks.
  • ☐Control microbial quality of the raw material and use a robust preservative system: oat is a natural substrate for microbes.
  • ☐Combine with barrier lipids such as ceramides or shea for dry and eczema-prone skin products. Note gluten and oat allergy questions on labels and in consumer care answers.

05Regulatory and claims

In the US, colloidal oatmeal is a skin protectant active in OTC Monograph M016 (21 CFR 347.10) at a minimum of 0.007%, or 0.003% with mineral oil. Monograph products may state that they "temporarily protect and help relieve minor skin irritation and itching due to rashes, eczema, poison ivy, oak or sumac, or insect bites", but must then follow OTC drug rules. Outside that route, and in other markets, claims stay cosmetic. CSL regulatory specialists confirm the status in each target market.

06Questions brands ask

Is colloidal oatmeal the same as oat extract?

Not exactly. Colloidal oatmeal is the whole milled kernel and is the form defined in the US monograph. Oat extracts contain only some components and have separate, smaller evidence.

Can a product say it helps eczema?

In the US only as an OTC skin protectant that follows the monograph. As a cosmetic, say "suitable for dry, eczema-prone skin" or "soothes dry, itchy-feeling skin".

Is it safe for babies and children?

Trials included children from 6 months, with few side effects. People with a known oat allergy should avoid it.
