Glossary · Regulatory

CMR substances

What it means

Substances classified as carcinogenic, mutagenic or toxic for reproduction. Under Article 15 of the EU Cosmetics Regulation they are prohibited in cosmetics unless a specific exemption applies, and newly classified CMRs are added to Annex II through regular omnibus acts.

Sources: 12

Why it matters

Missing or out-of-date regulatory work is one of the most common reasons a product is held at the border or withdrawn after launch.

How Guires CSL applies it

Guires CSL formulation, testing, clinical and regulatory teams work with CMR substances on client projects every week, from the first brief to the final product file.

Related terms

More in regulatory

PIF (Product Information File)

The technical file required under Article 11 of the EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009, held by the Responsible Person for ten years after the last batch is placed on the market.

Read definition →

CPNP (Cosmetic Products Notification Portal)

The EU online portal where every cosmetic must be notified before it is placed on the market, under Article 13 of Regulation 1223/2009..

Read definition →

Responsible Person

The legal or natural person, established in the EU (or in Great Britain for the UK), who is responsible for making sure each cosmetic complies with the regulation, holds the PIF and notifies the product..

Read definition →

Margin of Safety (MoS)

The ratio between a toxicological point of departure, such as a NOAEL, and the systemic exposure from using the product.

Read definition →
