The science

The science of young skin.

A baby’s skin barrier keeps maturing for the first years of life. We design around three areas.

Gentle cleansing

Clean skin, no tears.

Newborn and baby skin is thinner and loses water faster. Mild washes at skin-friendly pH clean without disrupting the barrier, and published trials show a well-formulated baby wash can be as kind to skin as water alone.

Formulation note

No-tears claims rely on the surfactant system and pH. We screen mildness in vitro before any in-use study with babies.

Moisture & barrier

Soft skin, honest claims.

Emollients help dry baby skin feel comfortable. Early small trials suggested daily emollients from birth might prevent eczema, but two large trials did not confirm it. We build moisturisers that care for dry skin and avoid prevention claims the evidence does not support.

Evidence note

We show both positive and negative trials here. Claims like “prevents eczema” are not supported for cosmetic emollients and would make the product a medicine.

Nappy area & sun

Protection where it matters most.

The nappy area is warm, wet and in contact with irritants. Barrier creams with zinc oxide and dexpanthenol help protect it. For sun, mineral sunscreens for children are designed for high protection with minimal irritation.

Clinical evidence

What the published trials show.

Published trials on cleansing, moisturising and protecting baby skin, including large trials with negative results. Each links to its record.

Randomised controlled trialMild cleansing · infants

Alkaline soap vs syndets vs water

Open, randomised, controlled · 40 infants

Alkaline soap raised skin pH most (+0.45 vs +0.19 for water) and removed the most surface lipid; syndets were gentler.

Gfatter R et al. Dermatology 1997;195(3):258–262 · PMID 9407174 ↗

Randomised controlled trialGentle wash · newborn barrier

Baby wash vs water only

Assessor-blinded, randomised non-inferiority · 307 newborns · 14–28 days

The wash was non-inferior to water on TEWL (12.8 vs 12.5 g/m²/h) and gave higher skin hydration (p=0.022).

Lavender T et al. J Obstet Gynecol Neonatal Nurs 2013;42(2):203–214 · PMID 23421327 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBaby wipes vs water

Alcohol-free baby wipe vs cotton wool and water

Assessor-blinded equivalence trial · 280 newborns · 4 weeks

Wipes were equivalent to water on skin hydration, and mother-reported nappy rash was lower with wipes (p=0.025).

Industry-funded.

Lavender T et al. BMC Pediatr 2012;12:59 · PMID 22656391 ↗

Randomised controlled trialEmollient from birth

Daily full-body emollient from birth vs none

Randomised, assessor-blinded · 124 high-risk newborns · 6 months

Eczema at 6 months was 22% vs 43% (RR 0.50).

Small trial; larger trials below did not confirm prevention.

Simpson EL et al. J Allergy Clin Immunol 2014;134(4):818–823 · PMID 25282563 ↗

Randomised controlled trialEmollient from birth

Daily moisturiser for 32 weeks vs control

Randomised · 118 high-risk newborns · 32 weeks

About 32% fewer moisturised infants developed eczema by week 32 (P=.012).

Horimukai K et al. J Allergy Clin Immunol 2014;134(4):824–830 · PMID 25282564 ↗

Randomised controlled trialEmollient from birth · large trial

Daily emollient for the first year vs advice only

Multicentre, pragmatic · 1,394 newborns · 2 years

No reduction in eczema at age 2 (23% vs 25%), and more skin infections in the emollient group.

Negative trial: we do not claim emollients prevent eczema.

Chalmers JR et al. Lancet 2020;395(10228):962–972 · PMID 32087126 ↗

Randomised controlled trialEmollient from birth · large trial

Emollient oil baths and face cream vs no intervention

Cluster-randomised factorial · 2,397 newborns · 12 months

Skin care did not reduce atopic dermatitis (11% vs 8%).

Negative trial.

Skjerven HO et al. Lancet 2020;395(10228):951–961 · PMID 32087121 ↗

Randomised controlled trialDry, eczema-prone skin · coconut oil

Virgin coconut oil vs mineral oil

Randomised, double-blind · 117 children · 8 weeks

Eczema severity fell 68% with coconut oil vs 38% with mineral oil (P<0.05).

Evangelista MT et al. Int J Dermatol 2014;53(1):100–108 · PMID 24320105 ↗

Randomised controlled trialEczema-prone skin · colloidal oatmeal

1% colloidal oatmeal cream vs prescription barrier cream

Randomised, double-blind, active-controlled · children with eczema · 3 weeks

The oatmeal cream matched the prescription barrier cream on eczema improvement, with similar tolerability.

Manufacturer-sponsored.

Lisante TA et al. J Dermatolog Treat 2017;28(7):659–667 · PMID 28366039 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBaby oils on newborn skin

Olive oil vs sunflower oil vs no oil

Randomised, assessor-blinded pilot · 115 newborns · 4 weeks

Both oils raised hydration but gave less improvement in skin lipid structure than no oil, a reason to test oils carefully on newborn skin.

Cooke A et al. Acta Derm Venereol 2016;96(3):323–330 ↗

Controlled trialNappy care · dexpanthenol + zinc oxide

Dexpanthenol and zinc oxide ointment vs base

Split-side comparison · 46 children with nappy rash · 7 days

Barrier function (TEWL) was better on the treated side by day 3 (p=0.002); severity scores did not differ.

Wananukul S et al. J Med Assoc Thai 2006;89(10):1654–1658 · PMID 17128840 ↗

These are published studies on ingredients, methods and routines, not on products we have made. A finished product needs its own testing before a claim goes on pack, and we design that study with you. See the full evidence library

Claims review
Cosmetic or drug?

In the EU the safety assessment must consider children under three specifically, and some ingredients are not allowed for them. “Treats nappy rash” or “prevents eczema” are medicine claims; “protects” and “soothes” are cosmetic. Kids’ sunscreen is an OTC drug in the US.

FAQ

Baby & kids questions.

Can we claim our emollient prevents eczema?

No. Two large trials found daily emollients from birth did not prevent eczema, and a prevention claim would make the product a medicine. We help you word care claims the evidence supports.

Are natural oils safe for newborns?

Not automatically. Some plant oils can affect the developing skin barrier. We choose and test oils rather than assume they are gentle.

How are baby products tested?

We start with in-vitro mildness screens, then patch tests on adults, then paediatrician- or dermatologist-supervised in-use studies on children with ethics approval.

Who owns the formula?

You do. We sign an NDA first, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end.
