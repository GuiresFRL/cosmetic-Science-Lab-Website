Glycolic acid: resurfacing, brightening and the evidence behind it

The smallest alpha hydroxy acid (AHA) penetrates fastest, which makes it the most effective for texture and the most likely to irritate. Here is what trials show and how strength, pH and labelling differ by market.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Good for texture and photoageing; peel evidence does not transfer to retail strengths

At a glance

What it is
An alpha hydroxy acid (AHA) from sugar cane or synthesis; molecular weight 76
Main benefits
Smoother texture, brighter-looking skin, appearance of photoageing
Levels tested
8% leave-on creams; 35–70% in-clinic peels
Time to results
Weeks for texture; months for photoageing
Watch for
Irritation, sun sensitivity, market limits on % and pH

01What is it?

Glycolic acid loosens the bonds between dead cells on the skin surface so they shed more evenly. It is used in leave-on serums and toners at low strength, and in professional peels at much higher strength.

02What it does

By speeding up shedding of the outer layer, glycolic acid smooths texture and lets skin reflect light more evenly. Longer use at higher strength has also been linked to thicker, denser skin in biopsy studies (Ditre 1996).

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceGood for texture and photoageing; peel evidence does not transfer to retail strengths

CSL verdict

Glycolic acid is the best-supported AHA for texture and a well-proven peel. Most recent pigmentation data come from in-clinic peels at 35–70%, which should not be used to support claims for a retail product.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Retail leave-on strength is limited by market (see below); trial benefits for photodamage were shown at 8% in a leave-on cream.
  • ☐pH matters as much as percentage: lower pH means more free acid, more effect and more irritation.
  • ☐Pair it with sunscreen advice and, for tone, with a brightening active; alone it mainly improves texture.
  • ☐For sensitive skin, lactic acid or mandelic acid is usually the better choice.

05Regulatory and claims

AHAs have clear limits. In the US, the Cosmetic Ingredient Review found AHAs safe in retail products at 10% or less with a final pH of 3.5 or higher, and FDA recommends a “Sunburn Alert” label because AHAs raise UV sensitivity (about 18% more reddening after 4 weeks in FDA-sponsored studies). In the EU, the SCCNFP advised precautionary consumer limits of 4% at pH 3.8 or higher for glycolic acid and 2.5% at pH 5 or higher for lactic acid, with warnings to avoid the eyes and protect from UV. CSL regulatory specialists set concentration, pH and warnings for each target market.

06Questions brands ask

Is glycolic acid safe for daily use?

At retail strengths within market limits, yes for most skin, with sunscreen. Start low and build up.

Why is it the most irritating AHA?

It is the smallest AHA, so it penetrates fastest. That helps results but raises irritation.

Do peel results apply to serums?

No. Peel trials use 35–70% in clinic. Retail serums are far weaker and need their own evidence.
