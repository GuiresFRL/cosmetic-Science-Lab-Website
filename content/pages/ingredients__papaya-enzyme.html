Papaya enzyme (papain): gentle exfoliation, and what the evidence does not yet show

Papain is a popular “natural exfoliant”, but controlled human studies on the face are missing. Here is what is known, the allergy risk to manage, and how CSL formulation scientists use it responsibly.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Limited

At a glance

What it is
Papain, a protein-digesting enzyme from papaya (Carica papaya)
Main benefits
Gentle enzymatic exfoliation; smoother-feeling skin
Levels
Set by enzyme activity, not by percentage
Human evidence
Very limited: no controlled facial trial of papain alone
Watch for
Allergy (a known IgE allergen), activity loss in water-based formulas

01What is it?

Papain breaks down proteins, including the keratin that holds dead cells together on the skin surface. It is sold as purified papain or as papaya fruit extract, which is not the same thing: plain fruit extract may have little enzyme activity.

02What it does

In the laboratory, papain broke down skin proteins such as keratin and collagen, supporting an exfoliating effect (Trevisol 2022). It needs the right conditions to work and loses much of its activity at skin temperature and acidic pH.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceLimited: laboratory studies and one combination-product trial; no facial RCT of papain alone

CSL verdict

Papain can support gentle, cosmetic exfoliation, but there is no controlled human evidence for brightening. CSL recommends texture and “smoother-feeling skin” claims only, rinse-off formats, and a product-specific in-use study before launch.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Specify enzyme activity units on the certificate of analysis, not just “papaya extract”.
  • ☐Enzymes lose activity in water: consider powders, anhydrous formats or encapsulation, and run real-time activity stability tests.
  • ☐Prefer rinse-off products with short contact time; avoid use on broken or eczema-prone skin.
  • ☐Control dust in manufacturing: papain powder is a known cause of occupational allergy.

05Regulatory and claims

Papaya fruit ingredients have a “safe as used” conclusion from the US Cosmetic Ingredient Review at low levels; papain was not included and is a known allergen. CSL regulatory specialists add allergy guidance where needed and limit claims to cosmetic exfoliation.

06Questions brands ask

Does papaya enzyme brighten skin?

There is no controlled human evidence that it does. It can smooth the surface, which may make skin look fresher.

Is it gentler than acids?

It can be, in rinse-off products, but papain is an allergen and can irritate damaged skin.

Is papaya extract the same as papain?

No. Fruit extracts may contain little active enzyme. Buy against a stated activity.
