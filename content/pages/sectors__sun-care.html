The science

The photobiology we formulate for.

Sun care briefs come down to three things: protection that matches the label, a texture people apply generously, and protection beyond UVB.

UV protection

Protection that matches the label.

Daily sunscreen use is one of the best-evidenced interventions in dermatology, reducing skin cancer and visible ageing in long-term randomised trials. Delivering that benefit starts with a filter system that reaches its SPF and UVA targets under each market’s rules.

Market note

UV filter lists differ between the EU, US, Australia and ASEAN, and sunscreens are cosmetics in some markets and drugs or therapeutic goods in others. We pick a filter system that works for your whole launch list.

Wearability

A texture that gets applied properly.

SPF is tested at 2 mg/cm², but most people apply far less, and protection drops steeply when they do. Light, fast-absorbing, invisible textures are how a sunscreen delivers its labelled protection in real life.

Formulation note

Mineral filters and high SPF pull textures towards heavy and white. We balance particle size, dispersion and emollients so the product stays elegant on deeper skin tones.

Beyond UVB

Protection for visible light and sensitive skin.

Visible light can worsen pigmentation such as melasma, especially in deeper skin tones. Tinted sunscreens with iron oxides block part of it. Lip SPF, sensitive-skin and after-sun products complete the range.

Clinical evidence

What the published trials show.

Peer-reviewed human studies behind the ingredients and approaches on this page. Each links to its PubMed record.

Randomised controlled trialMelanoma · daily sunscreen

Daily SPF 15+ sunscreen vs discretionary use

Randomised trial · 1,621 adults · 4.5 years + 10-year follow-up

Invasive melanomas were fewer with daily use (3 vs 11; HR 0.27, 95% CI 0.08–0.97); all new primary melanomas halved (11 vs 22), borderline significant.

Green AC et al. J Clin Oncol 2011;29(3):257–263 · PMID 21135266 ↗

Randomised controlled trialSkin cancer · daily sunscreen

Daily SPF 15+ sunscreen vs discretionary use

Community randomised factorial trial · 1,621 adults · 4.5 years

Squamous cell carcinoma tumours were 39% lower with daily sunscreen (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.46–0.81); no effect on basal cell carcinoma.

Green A et al. Lancet 1999;354(9180):723–729 · PMID 10475183 ↗

Randomised controlled trialPhotoageing · daily sunscreen

Daily broad-spectrum SPF 15+ vs discretionary use

Randomised community trial, blinded assessment · 903 adults · 4.5 years

Daily users showed 24% less skin ageing (relative odds 0.76) than discretionary users.

Hughes MC et al. Ann Intern Med 2013;158(11):781–790 · PMID 23732711 ↗

Randomised controlled trialSun damage · actinic keratoses

SPF 17 sunscreen vs base cream

Randomised, placebo-controlled · 588 adults aged 40+ · one summer

Fewer new solar keratoses with sunscreen (1.6 vs 2.3 per person), and more existing lesions went into remission.

Thompson SC et al. N Engl J Med 1993;329(16):1147–1151 · PMID 8377777 ↗

Randomised controlled trialHow people use SPF

SPF 30 vs SPF 10 on holiday

Randomised, double-blind · 87 young adults · summer holiday

People used the same amount of sunscreen but stayed in the sun longer with SPF 30 (72.6 vs 58.2 hours), a reason to design for real use.

Autier P et al. J Natl Cancer Inst 1999;91(15):1304–1309 · PMID 10433619 ↗

Controlled trialApplication amount vs SPF

Sunscreen at 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 mg/cm² on the same volunteers

In-vivo SPF study · 20 volunteers

Protection fell steeply below the 2 mg/cm² test dose, so texture that encourages generous use matters as much as the SPF number.

Faurschou A, Wulf HC. Br J Dermatol 2007;156(4):716–719 · PMID 17493070 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMelasma · visible-light protection

UV + visible-light sunscreen with iron oxides vs UV-only sunscreen

Randomised, double-blind · 68 patients with melasma · 8 weeks

The iron-oxide formula improved melasma severity by 15% more than the UV-only sunscreen (MASI), with lower melanin on biopsy.

Castanedo-Cazares JP et al. Photodermatol Photoimmunol Photomed 2014;30(1):35–42 · PMID 24313385 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMelasma relapse · tinted SPF

Tinted UV + visible-light sunscreen vs UV-only sunscreen

Randomised comparative trial · 40 patients · 6 months

Melasma relapsed far less with the tinted, visible-light formula (median MASI rise 0.45 vs 2.43, P=.027).

Boukari F et al. J Am Acad Dermatol 2015;72(1):189–190 · PMID 25443629 ↗

Randomised controlled trialLip SPF

Lip sunscreen vs placebo before UV exposure

Double-blind crossover · 38 people prone to cold sores

UV-triggered cold sores appeared in 71% after placebo and in none after sunscreen.

Rooney JF et al. Lancet 1991;338(8780):1419–1422 · PMID 1683420 ↗

FAQ

Sun care questions.

Can one sunscreen formula be sold in the EU and the US?

Sometimes, but the approved UV filter lists differ and the US treats sunscreen as an OTC drug. We often design one base with market-specific filter systems.

Where is SPF testing done?

In accredited laboratories using ISO 24444 for SPF, ISO 24443 for UVA and standard water-resistance protocols.

Can you make a mineral sunscreen without white cast?

Yes. We control particle size, dispersion and tint, and test on panels across skin tones.

Can we claim protection from blue light or visible light?

Only with data. We measure visible-light transmission and word the claim separately from the SPF rating.

Who owns the formula?

You do. We sign an NDA before you share your brief, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end.
