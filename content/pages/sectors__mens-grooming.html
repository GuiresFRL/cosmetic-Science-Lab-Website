The science

The skin and hair biology of men’s grooming.

Male skin tends to be oilier, and daily shaving stresses its barrier. We design around three areas.

Shave comfort

A closer shave, a calmer face.

Shaving removes hair and part of the skin’s surface each time. Good shave preparations lift and soften hair, add glide and leave the barrier intact; after-shave care calms redness and helps prevent ingrown hairs, especially for coarse, curly beard hair.

Claims note

“Reduces the look of razor bumps” is cosmetic; “treats pseudofolliculitis” is medicinal. We word the claim for each market.

Oil & shine control

Less shine, clearer-looking skin.

Higher sebum output means shine, visible pores and blemishes for many men. Oil-control face washes and matte moisturisers reduce shine without stripping, and gentle actives help blemish-prone skin.

Beard & hair

A softer beard, healthy skin beneath.

Beard hair is coarser than scalp hair and the skin under it is prone to itch and flaking. Beard oils, balms and washes soften the hair and care for the skin in one step.

Clinical evidence

What the published trials show.

Published human studies on shaving, oil control and cleansing. Some are method examples rather than product trials, and we label them that way.

Randomised controlled trialOil control · niacinamide

2% niacinamide vs placebo

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled · 100 adults · 4 weeks (plus a 30-person split-face study)

Sebum excretion fell significantly vs placebo at 2 and 4 weeks; casual sebum also fell in the split-face study.

Draelos ZD et al. J Cosmet Laser Ther 2006;8(2):96–101 · PMID 16766489 ↗

Randomised controlled trialRazor bumps · shaving routine

Shaving frequency and an advanced shave regimen vs standard daily shaving

Randomised, three parallel groups · 90 men with razor bumps · 12 weeks

The advanced daily regimen improved itching (P=.002) and perceived results; shaving less often reduced ingrown hairs.

Daniel A et al. J Drugs Dermatol 2013;12(4):410–418 · PMID 23652888 ↗

Published studyShaving irritation · razor design

Single-blade vs three-blade cartridge razor

Within-subject comparison with near-infrared imaging · 59 men

Redness right after shaving was lower with the single-blade razor (40% vs 58%), showing how imaging can quantify shave comfort.

Not randomised; shown as a method example.

Boodoo et al. Skin Res Technol 2024;30:e13598 · PMID 38279588 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAcne face wash · tolerance

Reformulated 4% and 10% benzoyl peroxide washes vs an older wash

Randomised, evaluator-blind · 133 adults with acne · 21 days

The new washes were as well tolerated as the reference, with no meaningful change in irritation scores.

Benzoyl peroxide acne washes are OTC drugs in the US and medicines in the EU.

Santos-Caetano JP, Cargill MR. J Drugs Dermatol 2019;18(4):350–356 ↗

These are published studies on ingredients, methods and routines, not on products we have made. A finished product needs its own testing before a claim goes on pack, and we design that study with you. See the full evidence library

Claims review
Cosmetic or drug?

Shaving, cleansing and “helps reduce the look of shine or redness” are cosmetic claims. Treating acne, razor bumps or folliculitis is a medicine claim, and benzoyl peroxide is a drug active in the US and restricted in EU cosmetics.

FAQ

Men's grooming questions.

Is men’s skin really different?

On average it produces more sebum and is stressed by daily shaving. We test on male panels so the results reflect the men who will use the product.

Can you develop products for razor bumps in coarse or curly beards?

Yes. We combine shave preparations, gentle exfoliants and calming actives, and test on panels with the relevant hair types.

Can one product be a wash, shampoo and body wash?

Yes. We design mild multi-use washes and test them on hair and skin.

Who owns the formula?

You do. We sign an NDA first, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end.
