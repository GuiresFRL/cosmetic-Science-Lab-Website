Rosemary for hair: what the evidence shows for thinning and hair fall

Rosemary oil is one of the most talked-about hair ingredients, yet it rests on one human trial. Here is what that trial showed, its limits, and how CSL formulation scientists use rosemary responsibly.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Limited

At a glance

What it is
Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis) essential oil or leaf extract
Main benefits
Scalp care and fuller-looking hair; a supporting role in anti-hair-fall formulas
Levels tested in trials
Rosemary oil standardised to at least 20 mg cineole per mL, applied twice daily; animal work used leaf extract
Time to results
No change at 3 months; change seen at 6 months in the only trial
Watch for
Over-reliance on one trial; essential-oil irritation and fragrance allergens

01What is it?

Rosemary is a culinary herb. In hair care it appears as an essential oil or as a leaf extract, which are chemically different. Its fame comes largely from one 2015 trial and later social media, not from a large body of research.

02What it does

In mice, rosemary leaf extract blocked 5-alpha-reductase, the enzyme that makes the hormone DHT, and supported regrowth after testosterone treatment. In the one human trial, men using rosemary oil saw hair counts rise over 6 months, much like the group using 2% minoxidil. Whether rosemary itself caused this, rather than time or massage, is not proven.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceLimited: one single-blind comparative trial with no placebo, plus animal and lab studies

CSL verdict

Rosemary has plausible lab and animal data and one small human trial, but no placebo-controlled evidence. It fits well as a supporting botanical, with claims kept to 'nourishes the scalp' or 'hair looks fuller', backed by testing of the finished product. Comparisons with minoxidil should not be used in marketing.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Decide early between essential oil and leaf extract: they differ in chemistry, odour and safety profile, and the only human trial used the oil.
  • ☐Keep essential-oil levels low in leave-on scalp products and run a patch test; CSL formulation scientists check fragrance allergen content from the supplier's data.
  • ☐Use rosemary alongside better-studied actives such as caffeine, and test the finished formula for hair fall or fullness claims.
  • ☐Protect oils from light and air, and include an antioxidant to limit oxidation of terpenes.

05Regulatory and claims

Rosemary oil and extract are widely used in cosmetics. Essential oils contain fragrance allergens that may need to be declared on the label in the EU; CSL regulatory specialists check the exact limits and labelling in each target market. Claims such as 'regrows hair' or 'as effective as minoxidil' are medical claims and must not be used on a cosmetic.

06Questions brands ask

Is rosemary oil as good as minoxidil?

One 2015 trial found similar hair-count gains at 6 months with rosemary oil and 2% minoxidil, but it had no placebo group and was only single-blind. That is not enough to make any comparison claim.

Is rosemary extract the same as rosemary oil?

No. The only human trial used the essential oil; animal work used a leaf extract. Claims should match the form tested.

Is rosemary safe on the scalp?

It was well tolerated in the trial, with less itching than minoxidil. Essential oils can still irritate or sensitise, so levels and allergens need checking.
