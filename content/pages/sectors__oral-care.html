The science

The oral science we formulate for.

Oral care has decades of randomised trials and Cochrane reviews behind it. We design around three areas.

Cavity protection

Stronger enamel, fewer cavities.

Fluoride toothpaste is one of the best-evidenced preventive products there is. Fluoride-free options such as hydroxyapatite now have randomised trials showing non-inferiority, and xylitol may add a small benefit.

  • AbrasivityRDA and REA testing
Published evidenceWalsh 2019 · fluoride toothpastePaszynska 2023 · hydroxyapatiteRiley 2015 · xylitol
Market note

Fluoride is capped at 1,500 ppm in EU cosmetics; in the US, anticavity toothpaste is an OTC drug with fixed actives and wording; India caps available fluoride at 1,000 ppm under BIS. We formulate to each limit.

Gums & sensitivity

Healthy gums, comfortable teeth.

Stannous fluoride and cetylpyridinium chloride reduce plaque and gum bleeding in randomised trials. For sensitivity, arginine and potassium work by different mechanisms, blocking tubules or calming nerves.

Formulation note

Stannous, zinc and CPC taste bitter or astringent. We work with flavour-house partners to mask them without losing efficacy.

Whitening & breath

Brighter smiles, fresher breath.

Peroxide whitening works, but consumer strength is tightly limited in some markets. Non-peroxide stain removal fills the gap. For breath, zinc salts neutralise the sulphur gases behind bad breath.

In the EU, consumer products may release no more than 0.1% hydrogen peroxide; 0.1–6% is for dental professionals only. We design whitening to fit each market.

Clinical evidence

What the published trials show.

Randomised trials and Cochrane systematic reviews on the actives we formulate with. Several are industry-funded, and we say so.

Systematic reviewCaries · fluoride toothpaste

Fluoride toothpastes of different strengths vs non-fluoride

Cochrane network meta-analysis · 96 RCTs · ~65,000 participants

1,450–1,500 ppm fluoride clearly reduced decay vs non-fluoride paste in children and adolescents, and slightly outperformed 1,000–1,250 ppm.

Walsh T et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2019;3:CD007868 · PMID 30829399 ↗

Randomised controlled trialCaries · hydroxyapatite

Fluoride-free hydroxyapatite toothpaste vs 1,450 ppm fluoride

Randomised, double-blind non-inferiority · 194 adults · 18 months

No caries increase in 89% (hydroxyapatite) vs 87% (fluoride); hydroxyapatite was non-inferior.

Co-authors linked to a manufacturer.

Paszynska E et al. Front Public Health 2023;11:1199728 ↗

Systematic reviewCaries · xylitol

Xylitol products for caries prevention

Cochrane systematic review · 10 studies · 5,903 participants

Fluoride toothpaste with 10% xylitol may reduce caries by 13% more than fluoride alone; evidence is low certainty.

Riley P et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2015;(3):CD010743 · PMID 25809586 ↗

Randomised controlled trialGum health · stannous fluoride

0.454% stannous fluoride toothpaste vs standard fluoride

Randomised, examiner-blind · 130 adults · 3 weeks

Gum bleeding was 29% lower than control at week 3 (p<0.0001).

Industry-funded.

Acherkouk A et al. BMC Oral Health 2021;21:441 · PMID 34511098 ↗

Randomised controlled trialPlaque & gums · CPC mouthwash

0.07% cetylpyridinium chloride rinse vs placebo

Randomised, placebo-controlled · 139 adults · 6 months

15% less gum inflammation, 33% less bleeding and 16% less plaque than placebo.

Industry-sponsored.

Mankodi S et al. Am J Dent 2005;18(Spec No):9A–14A · PMID 16178130 ↗

Randomised controlled trialSensitivity · arginine

8% arginine toothpaste regimen vs potassium regimen vs control

Randomised, double-blind, three-arm · 118 adults · 8 weeks

Air-blast sensitivity fell 70% vs negative control and 50% vs the potassium regimen.

Regimen included a mouthwash.

Elias Boneta AR et al. J Dent 2013;41(Suppl 1):S42–S49 ↗

Systematic reviewSensitivity · potassium

Potassium toothpastes vs control

Cochrane systematic review · 6 trials pooled

Tactile/air-blast sensitivity improved at 6–8 weeks, but self-reported benefit was not significant.

Poulsen S et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2006;(3):CD001476 · PMID 16855970 ↗

Randomised controlled trialWhitening · peroxide strips

6% hydrogen peroxide strips vs placebo strips

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled · 40 adults · 6 weeks

Tooth yellowness fell significantly more than placebo by week 2 (p<0.001), with continued gains to week 6.

6% peroxide is dentist-only in the EU; consumer products are limited to 0.1%.

Swift EJ Jr et al. J Dent 2009;37:e51–e56 · PMID 19523738 ↗

Randomised controlled trialFresh breath · zinc

Zinc chloride + o-cymen-5-ol toothpaste vs standard fluoride

Examiner-blind crossover · 75 adults · 1 week

Odour-causing sulphur gases were lower at every point up to 12 hours (p<0.0001), down 91% immediately after brushing.

Industry study.

Payne D et al. Int Dent J 2011;61(Suppl 3):60–66 · PMID 21762157 ↗

Systematic reviewHerbal toothpaste

Herbal vs conventional toothpastes and rinses

Systematic review and meta-analysis · 24 RCTs

Herbal toothpaste reduced plaque more at 4 weeks, but showed no advantage for gum health or over fluoride toothpaste.

Janakiram C et al. BMC Complement Med Ther 2020;20:43 · PMID 32046707 ↗

These are published studies on ingredients, methods and routines, not on products we have made. A finished product needs its own testing before a claim goes on pack, and we design that study with you. See the full evidence library

Cosmetic or drug?

Toothpaste and mouthwash are cosmetics in the EU and India, but anticavity, anti-gingivitis and anti-sensitivity claims make them OTC drugs in the US. EU limits fluoride to 1,500 ppm and consumer hydrogen peroxide to 0.1%.

FAQ

Oral care questions.

Can you make a fluoride-free toothpaste that works?

Yes. Hydroxyapatite toothpaste was non-inferior to fluoride for caries in an 18-month randomised trial. We formulate with it and support the claim.

Can we sell a whitening product in the EU and US?

Yes, but EU consumer products are limited to 0.1% hydrogen peroxide. We design peroxide-free or low-peroxide whitening for the EU and stronger systems where allowed.

Do herbal toothpastes work?

A meta-analysis found herbal pastes reduced plaque at 4 weeks but did not beat fluoride pastes for gum health. We combine herbal actives with proven ones where you need the claim.

Who owns the formula?

You do. We sign an NDA first, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end.
