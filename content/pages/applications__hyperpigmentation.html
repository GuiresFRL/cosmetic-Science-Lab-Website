The science

How pigmentation forms, and how we target it.

Pigment is made in melanocytes, transferred to skin cells and triggered by UV and visible light. We work at each step.

Pigment production & transfer

Fewer dark spots, more even tone.

Niacinamide reduces the transfer of pigment to skin cells, and vitamin C interrupts pigment production. Both have vehicle-controlled trials showing visible improvement in spots and tone.

Evidence note

Topical tranexamic acid is popular, but we could not verify a vehicle-controlled trial showing a clear benefit on its own. We combine it with better-evidenced actives.

UV & visible light

Stop it coming back.

Visible light, not only UV, drives pigmentation in deeper skin tones. Randomised trials show tinted sunscreens with iron oxides improve melasma and reduce relapse compared with UV-only sunscreens.

Clinical evidence

What the published trials show.

Peer-reviewed human studies behind the actives and approaches on this page. Each links to its record.

Controlled trialEven tone · niacinamide

5% niacinamide vs vehicle; 2% niacinamide + sunscreen vs sunscreen

Vehicle-controlled human trials · 18 and 120 participants

Significantly less hyperpigmentation and lighter-looking skin vs vehicle, visible from 4 weeks.

Hakozaki T et al. Br J Dermatol 2002;147(1):20–31 · PMID 12100180 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · niacinamide

5% niacinamide vs vehicle, split-face

Double-blind, split-face · 50 women · 12 weeks

Significant reductions in fine lines, wrinkles, spots, red blotchiness and sallowness, and improved elasticity, vs the vehicle side.

Bissett DL et al. Dermatol Surg 2005;31(7 Pt 2):860–865 · PMID 16029679 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · vitamin C

5% vitamin C cream vs vehicle

Double-blind, randomised, split-site · 20 women · 6 months

Clinical photoageing score improved from 6.7 to 4.4 on the treated side, with denser skin microrelief and fewer deep furrows.

Humbert PG et al. Exp Dermatol 2003;12(3):237–244 · PMID 12823436 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMelasma · visible-light protection

UV + visible-light sunscreen with iron oxides vs UV-only sunscreen

Randomised, double-blind · 68 patients with melasma · 8 weeks

The iron-oxide formula improved melasma severity by 15% more than the UV-only sunscreen (MASI), with lower melanin on biopsy.

Castanedo-Cazares JP et al. Photodermatol Photoimmunol Photomed 2014;30(1):35–42 · PMID 24313385 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMelasma relapse · tinted SPF

Tinted UV + visible-light sunscreen vs UV-only sunscreen

Randomised comparative trial · 40 patients · 6 months

Melasma relapsed far less with the tinted, visible-light formula (median MASI rise 0.45 vs 2.43, P=.027).

Boukari F et al. J Am Acad Dermatol 2015;72(1):189–190 · PMID 25443629 ↗

These are published studies on ingredients, methods and routines, not on products we have made. A finished product needs its own testing before a claim goes on pack, and we design that study with you. See the full evidence library

FAQ

Hyperpigmentation & even tone questions.

Can we claim skin lightening?

We avoid “lightening” or “whitening”, which can suggest bleaching and are restricted in some markets. “Fades the look of dark spots” and “more even-looking tone” are supportable.

Is hydroquinone allowed?

Not in EU cosmetics, and it is restricted elsewhere. We build even-tone products on actives permitted in each market.

How long does an even-tone study take?

Usually 8 to 12 weeks, with colour measurement and imaging on panels that include the skin tones you sell to.

Who owns the formula?

You do. We sign an NDA before you share your brief, and the formula and technical file are handed over at the end of the project.
