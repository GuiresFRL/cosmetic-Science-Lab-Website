Modern organic UV filters: what the evidence shows for daily sunscreen

Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen is one of the best-proven ways to slow visible skin ageing. Here is the trial evidence, the new US status of bemotrizinol, and how CSL formulation scientists build modern filter systems.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Strong

At a glance

What it is
Carbon-based UV filters such as bemotrizinol (Tinosorb S), bisoctrizole (Tinosorb M) and avobenzone, used in combination
Main benefits
High SPF with balanced UVA cover in light textures; daily use slows visible sun ageing
Levels tested in trials
SPF 15+ with 8% octinoxate and 2% avobenzone in the Nambour trial; bemotrizinol 6% in US safety studies
Time to results
Sunburn protection is immediate; slower skin ageing was measured after 4.5 years of daily use
Watch for
Photostability, skin absorption of some older filters, correct application amount and market-by-market approval

01What is it?

Organic UV filters are carbon-based molecules that absorb UV light. Newer filters such as bemotrizinol and bisoctrizole are large, photostable molecules that cover both UVB and UVA. They have been used in Europe and other markets for years, and bemotrizinol was added to the US sunscreen monograph in 2026.

02What it does

Each filter absorbs a band of UV and releases the energy harmlessly as heat. Combining filters gives broad, even cover from UVB through long UVA. Used daily, this reduces sunburn and the cumulative UV damage that shows as wrinkles, roughness and uneven tone.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceStrong: large, long-term randomised trials show daily sunscreen reduces skin ageing and squamous-cell carcinoma; newer filters add safety and SPF data

CSL verdict

The case for daily broad-spectrum sunscreen is strong: long-term randomised trials show less visible skin ageing and fewer squamous-cell carcinomas. Newer filters such as bemotrizinol offer the same protection with very low skin absorption. Suitable cosmetic claims include “helps prevent visible signs of sun ageing with daily use” and “broad-spectrum UVA/UVB protection”, backed by SPF and UVA testing of the finished product.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Build a filter system, not a single filter: pair a photostable broad-spectrum filter such as bemotrizinol with UVB and long-UVA partners to hit the target SPF and UVA ratio.
  • ☐Stabilise avobenzone with partners such as bemotrizinol or octocrylene, and confirm photostability after irradiation.
  • ☐Check each filter against each launch market: bemotrizinol is now allowed in the US at up to 6%, but many EU-approved filters are still not permitted there.
  • ☐Formulate for daily wear: light textures, low stinging near the eyes and good spreadability improve how much people apply, which drives real protection.

05Regulatory and claims

In the EU, organic UV filters are cosmetic ingredients listed in Annex VI with maximum levels (bemotrizinol up to 10%). In the US, sunscreens are OTC drugs: FDA proposed adding bemotrizinol at up to 6% on 12 December 2025 and finalised it on 9 June 2026, the first new monograph filter in decades. Avobenzone and most older US filters were classed in FDA’s 2021 proposed order as needing more safety data. CSL regulatory specialists confirm allowed filters, levels, forms and label wording in each target market.

06Questions brands ask

Can US brands now use bemotrizinol?

Yes. FDA finalised bemotrizinol for OTC sunscreens up to 6% in June 2026. The US proposal excluded propellant aerosols and powders, so check the final order for the product form.

Are organic filters absorbed into the body?

Some older filters, such as oxybenzone and avobenzone, reach measurable blood levels. Bemotrizinol and bisoctrizole are large molecules with very low absorption.

Does daily sunscreen really slow ageing?

Yes. In a 4.5-year randomised trial, daily users showed 24% less skin ageing than people who used sunscreen when they chose.
