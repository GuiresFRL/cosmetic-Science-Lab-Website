Topical prebiotics: what the evidence shows for skin microbiome balance

Inulin and oligosaccharides are added to skin care to feed helpful skin bacteria. Human trials are few and small. Here is what they show and how CSL formulation scientists use prebiotics sensibly.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Limited

At a glance

What it is
Plant or dairy-derived sugars such as inulin, fructo-oligosaccharides (FOS), galacto-oligosaccharides (GOS) and alpha-glucan oligosaccharide
Main benefits
Support for a balanced skin microbiome; better hydration and barrier readings in small studies
Levels tested in trials
7% GOS in a serum; inulin and FOS in a gel cream (level not stated); 6 g/L inulin in a bath
Time to results
2 to 8 weeks in the studies available
Watch for
Few controlled trials; most studies use whole formulas, so the prebiotic's own effect is unclear

01What is it?

Prebiotics are sugars that some bacteria can use as food. In skin care the idea is to favour the skin's helpful bacteria over less welcome ones, without killing anything. Common choices are inulin, fructo-oligosaccharides and galacto-oligosaccharides. They are also humectants, so they help hold water in the skin.

02What it does

In lab work and small human studies, prebiotic formulas shifted the mix of skin bacteria and improved hydration and barrier readings. Some studies saw fewer acne spots or less redness. Because prebiotics are almost always tested inside a full moisturiser or cleanser, it is hard to say how much of the benefit comes from the prebiotic itself.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceLimited: a handful of small trials, often uncontrolled or industry-funded, testing whole formulas

Studies below were checked against the publisher, DOI or journal record on 26 Sep 2026. We list design and size so you can judge the strength of each result.

Source		Systematic review4 small trials of topical prebiotics in atopic-prone skin (22 to 60 people, 2 to 8 weeks)Two trials showed clinically relevant improvement and two showed statistically significant but small changes. The authors call the results promising but not yet conclusive.

CSL verdict

Prebiotics are gentle, safe humectants with an early but thin evidence base for microbiome support. The best controlled data are small, short trials of whole formulas. Suitable claims are modest, for example “supports the skin's natural balance” or “helps maintain a healthy-looking skin barrier”, backed by the finished product's own testing.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Typical use is a few per cent of inulin, FOS or GOS in the water phase. The only controlled leave-on trial found used 7% GOS; CSL formulation scientists treat that as a guide, not a rule.
  • ☐Prebiotic sugars are water-soluble, heat-tolerant and easy to combine with humectants, ceramides and gentle cleansers. They can feed spoilage microbes too, so preservative efficacy testing is essential.
  • ☐Any claim about the microbiome needs product-level evidence, such as a before-and-after microbiome study on the finished formula, not just supplier data.
  • ☐Prebiotics suit cleansers, toners and moisturisers for dry, sensitive or blemish-prone skin, where the aim is balance rather than a dramatic visible change.

05Regulatory and claims

Inulin, fructo-oligosaccharides and galacto-oligosaccharides are common cosmetic ingredients. CSL regulatory specialists confirm the status in each target market before a formula is finalised. Claims such as “microbiome-friendly” must be supported by evidence on the finished product. Keep wording cosmetic, for example “supports the skin's natural balance”, not “treats eczema” or “cures acne”.

06Questions brands ask

Do prebiotics change the skin microbiome?

Small studies suggest they can shift the mix of skin bacteria. Changes were measured in a few people over weeks, and it is not yet clear how long they last or how much they matter for how skin looks.

Are prebiotics the same as probiotics?

No. Prebiotics are sugars that feed bacteria. Probiotics are live bacteria. Postbiotics are inactivated bacteria or their by-products. Each has its own, separate evidence.

Will a prebiotic cream help with spots?

One open study of an inulin and FOS gel cream saw about a third fewer spots in 6 weeks, but it had no comparison group. It is not a substitute for proven spot-care actives.
