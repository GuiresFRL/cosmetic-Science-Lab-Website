Zinc pyrithione: evidence for dandruff and where it is still allowed

Zinc pyrithione was the benchmark anti-dandruff active for decades but has been banned in EU cosmetics since 2022. Here is the evidence, the rules, and how CSL formulation scientists use it where permitted.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Good

At a glance

What it is
A zinc complex of pyrithione with antifungal and antibacterial activity, used in anti-dandruff shampoos
Main benefits
Fewer visible flakes and less itch; long track record
Levels tested in trials
1% in most shampoo trials; US monograph range 0.3–2% rinse-off, 0.1–0.25% leave-on
Time to results
2 to 4 weeks in clinical studies
Watch for
Banned in EU and GB cosmetics; an OTC drug in the US; formula design drives results

01What is it?

Zinc pyrithione (ZPT) is a fine particle active that sits on the scalp after rinsing and acts against dandruff yeast. It was the most widely used anti-dandruff active worldwide. Since 1 March 2022 it has been banned in EU cosmetics after being classed as toxic for reproduction, but it remains an approved over-the-counter dandruff active in the US.

02What it does

In lab studies ZPT floods Malassezia yeast with zinc, disrupts its energy production and switches off the enzymes it uses to feed on scalp oil. On the scalp this shows as fewer visible flakes and less itch within weeks, with benefits fading once use stops.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceGood: decades of use and several randomised comparative trials, but few recent or placebo-controlled studies

CSL verdict

Zinc pyrithione reliably reduces dandruff at around 1% in shampoo, and remains a benchmark in comparative trials. How well it works depends heavily on how the formula delivers it. It can no longer be used in EU or GB cosmetics; elsewhere, check whether it is regulated as a cosmetic or a drug before making claims.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Confirm the target market first: ZPT cannot be placed on the EU market in cosmetics from 1 March 2022, or in Great Britain from 15 October 2022.
  • ☐For US products, use 0.3–2% in rinse-off or 0.1–0.25% in leave-on formulas, as set by the OTC monograph, with drug-facts labelling.
  • ☐ZPT is an insoluble particle: particle size, suspension stability and deposition aids decide how much stays on the scalp. A large trial showed an optimised ZPT base outperforming a two-active formula.
  • ☐Avoid strong chelators (such as EDTA) and iron contamination, which can discolour or destabilise ZPT; check colour and dispersion over shelf life.

05Regulatory and claims

Commission Regulation (EU) 2021/1902 added zinc pyrithione to Annex II (prohibited substances) of Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 from 1 March 2022, after its classification as toxic for reproduction (category 1B); Great Britain followed from 15 October 2022. In the US it is an OTC drug active for dandruff under 21 CFR 358.710 at 0.3–2% in wash-off and 0.1–0.25% in leave-on products. CSL regulatory specialists confirm the status in each other target market; in drug markets the permitted claims follow the monograph, while cosmetic wording stays at "helps reduce visible flakes".

06Questions brands ask

Can zinc pyrithione be used in products sold in the EU or UK?

No. It has been banned in EU cosmetics since 1 March 2022 and in Great Britain since 15 October 2022. Piroctone olamine and climbazole are the usual replacements.

Is zinc pyrithione still allowed in the US?

Yes, as an OTC drug active for dandruff at 0.3–2% in rinse-off and 0.1–0.25% in leave-on products, with drug labelling.

Is a higher percentage more effective?

Not necessarily. A 620-person trial showed that an optimised formula matters more than adding more actives, and most trials used 1%.
