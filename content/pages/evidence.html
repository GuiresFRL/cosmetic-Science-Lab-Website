Our evidence standard

How we choose what we cite.

Human trials first

We cite studies on people, not only lab or animal data, and prefer randomised, controlled, blinded designs.

Peer-reviewed and traceable

Every study is published in a peer-reviewed journal and linked to its PubMed record so you can read it yourself.

Labelled honestly

We say when a trial is open-label, compares against a medicine, or tests an oral supplement rather than a cosmetic.

Ingredient evidence is not product evidence

Published trials show what an ingredient or approach can do. A finished product still needs its own testing before a claim goes on pack.

Skin care

11 studies in skin care.

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · retinol

0.4% retinol lotion vs vehicle

Randomised, double-blind, vehicle-controlled · 36 older adults · 24 weeks

Fine wrinkling improved significantly vs vehicle (score change −1.64 vs −0.08, P<.001), with increased glycosaminoglycans and procollagen I.

Kafi R et al. Arch Dermatol 2007;143(5):606–612 · PMID 17515510 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · niacinamide

5% niacinamide vs vehicle, split-face

Double-blind, split-face · 50 women · 12 weeks

Significant reductions in fine lines, wrinkles, spots, red blotchiness and sallowness, and improved elasticity, vs the vehicle side.

Bissett DL et al. Dermatol Surg 2005;31(7 Pt 2):860–865 · PMID 16029679 ↗

Controlled trialEven tone · niacinamide

5% niacinamide vs vehicle; 2% niacinamide + sunscreen vs sunscreen

Vehicle-controlled human trials · 18 and 120 participants

Significantly less hyperpigmentation and lighter-looking skin vs vehicle, visible from 4 weeks.

Hakozaki T et al. Br J Dermatol 2002;147(1):20–31 · PMID 12100180 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · vitamin C

5% vitamin C cream vs vehicle

Double-blind, randomised, split-site · 20 women · 6 months

Clinical photoageing score improved from 6.7 to 4.4 on the treated side, with denser skin microrelief and fewer deep furrows.

Humbert PG et al. Exp Dermatol 2003;12(3):237–244 · PMID 12823436 ↗

Randomised controlled trialHydration · hyaluronic acid

0.1% hyaluronic acid creams (50–2000 kDa) vs vehicle

Randomised, controlled, intra-individual · 76 women · 60 days

All molecular weights improved hydration and elasticity vs vehicle; the low weights (50 and 130 kDa) also reduced wrinkle depth.

Pavicic T et al. J Drugs Dermatol 2011;10(9):990–1000 · PMID 22052267 ↗

Randomised controlled trialHydration · glycerin

20% glycerol emollient vs vehicle

Double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomised · 24 adults with atopic dermatitis · 4 weeks

Skin hydration rose vs vehicle from week 1 (p<0.0001) and barrier function (TEWL) improved by week 4.

Breternitz M et al. Skin Pharmacol Physiol 2008;21(1):39–45 · PMID 18025807 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBarrier · ceramides

Ceramide cream vs hydrophilic cream, split-body

Double-blind, randomised · 24 adults with dry skin · 28 days + 7-day follow-up

Better hydration, lower TEWL and improved skin pH at every time point, maintained 7 days after stopping.

Lueangarun S et al. Dermatol Ther 2019;32(6):e13090 · PMID 31585489 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMicrobiome · postbiotic

5% Vitreoscilla filiformis lysate cream vs vehicle

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled · 75 people with atopic dermatitis · 30 days

Reduced eczema severity (P=0.0044), itch and sleep loss, and lowered S. aureus colonisation vs placebo.

Gueniche A et al. Br J Dermatol 2008;159(6):1357–1363 · PMID 18795916 ↗

Randomised controlled trialBarrier · ectoin

Ectoin cream vs a non-steroidal barrier cream

Randomised, double-blind, intra-individual, multicentre · 65 adults · 28 days

Efficacy and tolerability equivalent to the active comparator on eczema score, global assessment and itch.

Marini A et al. Skin Pharmacol Physiol 2014;27(2):57–65 · PMID 23949258 ↗

Randomised controlled trialPhotoageing · daily sunscreen

Daily broad-spectrum SPF 15+ vs discretionary use

Randomised community trial, blinded assessment · 903 adults · 4.5 years

Daily users showed 24% less skin ageing (relative odds 0.76) than discretionary users.

Hughes MC et al. Ann Intern Med 2013;158(11):781–790 · PMID 23732711 ↗

Randomised controlled trialAnti-ageing · bakuchiol

0.5% bakuchiol vs 0.5% retinol

Randomised, double-blind · 44 adults · 12 weeks

Both reduced wrinkle area and hyperpigmentation with no significant difference; bakuchiol caused less scaling and stinging.

Randomised controlled trialMelanoma · daily sunscreen

Daily SPF 15+ sunscreen vs discretionary use

Randomised trial · 1,621 adults · 4.5 years + 10-year follow-up

Invasive melanomas were fewer with daily use (3 vs 11; HR 0.27, 95% CI 0.08–0.97); all new primary melanomas halved (11 vs 22), borderline significant.

Green AC et al. J Clin Oncol 2011;29(3):257–263 · PMID 21135266 ↗

Randomised controlled trialSkin cancer · daily sunscreen

Daily SPF 15+ sunscreen vs discretionary use

Community randomised factorial trial · 1,621 adults · 4.5 years

Squamous cell carcinoma tumours were 39% lower with daily sunscreen (RR 0.61, 95% CI 0.46–0.81); no effect on basal cell carcinoma.

Green A et al. Lancet 1999;354(9180):723–729 · PMID 10475183 ↗

Randomised controlled trialSun damage · actinic keratoses

SPF 17 sunscreen vs base cream

Randomised, placebo-controlled · 588 adults aged 40+ · one summer

Fewer new solar keratoses with sunscreen (1.6 vs 2.3 per person), and more existing lesions went into remission.

Thompson SC et al. N Engl J Med 1993;329(16):1147–1151 · PMID 8377777 ↗

Randomised controlled trialHow people use SPF

SPF 30 vs SPF 10 on holiday

Randomised, double-blind · 87 young adults · summer holiday

People used the same amount of sunscreen but stayed in the sun longer with SPF 30 (72.6 vs 58.2 hours), a reason to design for real use.

Autier P et al. J Natl Cancer Inst 1999;91(15):1304–1309 · PMID 10433619 ↗

Controlled trialApplication amount vs SPF

Sunscreen at 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 mg/cm² on the same volunteers

In-vivo SPF study · 20 volunteers

Protection fell steeply below the 2 mg/cm² test dose, so texture that encourages generous use matters as much as the SPF number.

Faurschou A, Wulf HC. Br J Dermatol 2007;156(4):716–719 · PMID 17493070 ↗

Randomised controlled trialMelasma relapse · tinted SPF

Tinted UV + visible-light sunscreen vs UV-only sunscreen

Randomised comparative trial · 40 patients · 6 months

Melasma relapsed far less with the tinted, visible-light formula (median MASI rise 0.45 vs 2.43, P=.027).

Rosemary oil vs 2% minoxidil

13 studies in personal care & hygiene.

Randomised controlled trialAntiperspirant · sweat reduction

20% aluminium chloride hexahydrate vs 1% aluminium acetate, one per underarm

Randomised split-axilla · 19 adults · gravimetric sweat test

The aluminium chloride side sweated significantly less than the comparison side (p=0.0002), showing how gravimetric testing separates actives.

Small study; 20% aluminium chloride is prescription strength in some markets.

Swary JH et al. J Cosmet Dermatol 2015;14(4) ↗

Published studyDeodorant · underarm microbiome

Stopping and restarting deodorant or antiperspirant vs non-users

Experimental study · 17 adults · 8 days · culture and 16S sequencing

Not randomised; no odour endpoint.

Urban J et al. PeerJ 2016;4:e1605 ↗

Randomised controlled trialHand hygiene · office workers

Alcohol hand disinfectant at desks vs usual practice

Randomised controlled trialHand hygiene · childcare

Randomised controlled trialHand care · barrier creams

Randomised controlled trialHand cream · healthcare workers

Systematic reviewCaries · fluoride toothpaste

Fluoride toothpastes of different strengths vs non-fluoride

Cochrane network meta-analysis · 96 RCTs · ~65,000 participants

1,450–1,500 ppm fluoride clearly reduced decay vs non-fluoride paste in children and adolescents, and slightly outperformed 1,000–1,250 ppm.

Fluoride-free hydroxyapatite toothpaste vs 1,450 ppm fluoride

Randomised, double-blind non-inferiority · 194 adults · 18 months

No caries increase in 89% (hydroxyapatite) vs 87% (fluoride); hydroxyapatite was non-inferior.

Co-authors linked to a manufacturer.

Paszynska E et al. Front Public Health 2023;11:1199728 ↗

Randomised controlled trialGum health · stannous fluoride

0.454% stannous fluoride toothpaste vs standard fluoride

Randomised, examiner-blind · 130 adults · 3 weeks

Gum bleeding was 29% lower than control at week 3 (p<0.0001).

Industry-funded.

Acherkouk A et al. BMC Oral Health 2021;21:441 · PMID 34511098 ↗

Randomised controlled trialSensitivity · arginine

8% arginine toothpaste regimen vs potassium regimen vs control

Randomised, double-blind, three-arm · 118 adults · 8 weeks

Air-blast sensitivity fell 70% vs negative control and 50% vs the potassium regimen.

Regimen included a mouthwash.

Elias Boneta AR et al. J Dent 2013;41(Suppl 1):S42–S49 ↗

Potassium toothpastes vs control

Cochrane systematic review · 6 trials pooled

Tactile/air-blast sensitivity improved at 6–8 weeks, but self-reported benefit was not significant.

6% hydrogen peroxide strips vs placebo strips

Randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled · 40 adults · 6 weeks

Tooth yellowness fell significantly more than placebo by week 2 (p<0.001), with continued gains to week 6.

6% peroxide is dentist-only in the EU; consumer products are limited to 0.1%.

Swift EJ Jr et al. J Dent 2009;37:e51–e56 · PMID 19523738 ↗

0.07% cetylpyridinium chloride rinse vs placebo

Randomised, placebo-controlled · 139 adults · 6 months

15% less gum inflammation, 33% less bleeding and 16% less plaque than placebo.

Industry-sponsored.

Systematic review and meta-analysis · 24 RCTs

Herbal toothpaste reduced plaque more at 4 weeks, but showed no advantage for gum health or over fluoride toothpaste.
