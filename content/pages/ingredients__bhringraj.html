Bhringraj for hair: what the evidence shows for hair fall and thinning

Bhringraj is a classic Ayurvedic hair herb. Animal studies are promising, but human data come only from multi-herb oils and small open studies. CSL formulation scientists explain what can honestly be claimed.

By CSL Formulation team · Evidence checked by CSL scientists

Ingredient guideEvidence: Limited

At a glance

What it is
Extract or infused oil of Eclipta alba (also called Eclipta prostrata), a traditional Ayurvedic herb
Main benefits
Traditional scalp and hair care; animal studies suggest support for the growth phase
Levels tested in trials
5% extract in animal studies; human studies used multi-herb oils or oral tablets at undisclosed levels
Time to results
4 months in multi-herb oil studies
Watch for
No human trial of bhringraj on its own; manufacturer-linked studies

01What is it?

Bhringraj is a small plant used for centuries in Ayurvedic hair oils. Cosmetic products use it as an extract or infused in an oil base, often with other herbs such as amla and indigo. Most research is in animals.

02What it does

In rats and mice, bhringraj extracts shortened the time to hair regrowth and increased the number of follicles in the growth phase, in one study more than 2% minoxidil. Human studies of multi-herb oils report less hair fall and more density, but they cannot show how much comes from bhringraj.

03The evidence

Strength of human evidenceLimited: animal studies only for the herb itself; human studies test multi-herb oils or are open-label

CSL verdict

Bhringraj has encouraging animal data and a long traditional use, but no human trial has tested it on its own. It suits Ayurvedic-style hair oils with claims such as 'traditional scalp care' or 'reduces hair fall due to breakage' only if the finished product is tested. It should not be compared with minoxidil in marketing.

04How CSL formulation scientists use it

  • ☐Specify the plant part, solvent and extract ratio; the animal studies used petroleum-ether and methanol extracts, which differ from oil infusions.
  • ☐Oil-infused bhringraj suits pre-wash oils; CSL formulation scientists check rancidity and add antioxidants to the oil base.
  • ☐Pair with better-studied actives if a performance claim is the goal, and test the finished formula over at least 3 to 4 months.
  • ☐Check botanical identity and contaminants (heavy metals, pesticides) in each supplier batch.

05Regulatory and claims

Eclipta extracts are used in cosmetics, and CSL regulatory specialists confirm the INCI name and status in each target market. In India, traditional oils may fall under Ayurvedic rules rather than cosmetic rules, depending on the claims. Claims such as 'regrows hair' or 'treats alopecia' are medical and not allowed on a cosmetic.

06Questions brands ask

Is there proof bhringraj stops hair fall?

Not on its own. Animal studies are positive, and multi-herb oils reduced hair fall in small human studies, but none isolated bhringraj.

Is bhringraj as good as minoxidil?

A rat study found it better, and one human trial of a multi-herb oil found similar density to minoxidil. Neither supports a comparison claim on a cosmetic.

Which form should a brand use?

It depends on the product. Traditional oils suit pre-wash treatments; standardised extracts are easier to dose in serums. Claims should match what the finished product has been tested for.
